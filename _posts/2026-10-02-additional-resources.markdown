Listed below are additional FRC resources from other teams or websites that I’ve found helpful during my time as a strategy lead. Continue to look online at resources provided by other developed teams, match breakdown videos, and of course ideas suggested by your own teammates.
[FRC Team 1678 (CitrusCircuits) fall workshop videos/presentations](https://www.youtube.com/@CitrusCircuits/playlists): [Game Analysis/Scoring Systems](https://youtu.be/EEF-r9oF0JY?si=o8Px_fh9lW1KxoRe), [The Picklist](https://youtu.be/R0hJnk5wxBw?si=bi9-Yut7MkycCgc1), and [Strategic Design]( https://youtu.be/G6X3SrMmX3g?si=jsL7PG5DVQESF3gj) are the ones in particular that I think are most relevant to the strategy subteam, but feel free to watch the others. The presentation they go over in each is linked in the description of each video.

[Fun Robotics Network](https://www.youtube.com/@FUNRoboticsNetwork): Youtube channel that posts lots of FRC content, from competition highlight reels, early-season strategy insights, to “behind-the-bumper” videos that take a closer look at high performing teams from around the world. 
  
[RoboReplay](https://www.youtube.com/@RoboReplay/videos): Another Youtube that gives detailed strategy breakdowns from competitions throughout the season. 

[Effective FIRST Strategies by Karthik Kanagasabapathy](https://youtu.be/cemXDEzBMhM?is=E5jv-SsXnuQHJVFz): A very long, detailed, and informational video about game analysis, data analysis, and bot design in FRC. 

[The Blue Alliance](https://www.thebluealliance.com/): A website and app that lists team and event information for official FIRST events. I would heavily suggest downloading the mobile app to have at competitions as it is a quick point of reference for the match schedule, rankings, team information. The statistics listed under the insights tab for the event can be a point of reference when comparing scouting data with other teams (or if you’re scouting data isn’t accurate/won’t load).

# GRITS
> *This article contains a quick overview of what the GRITS competition is, an overhead map of what the competition venue will look like, and a timeline for what each day looks like.*
## Overview
GRITS is an off-season competition, typically in early or mid October, that has no bearing on our placement during the real season that begins in January. GRITS will always play the game from the previous season (like a final hoo-rah to the bot you’ve been working on all year). It is a great opportunity for teams to experiment with their robot design and for new members to get a better understanding of what a competition looks like and what role they will play without the pressure of aiming to advance to the district or FIRST championship.

## Timeline
**Day 0:** The first day, usually a Thursday, is for teams to load into their pit area and get pre-inspected. Usually load-in opens at 5pm and the pits close at around 8 or 9pm. Not all team members will have to attend Day 0, in fact, it will probably just be some of the executive board members and adult mentors to assist with load-in and inspection. If you would like to attend Day 0, you will have to find your own transportation.


**Day 1:** The first day of official matches. Members will be expected to arrive when the doors open at 8am so we can have a spot in the stands for our team. Opening ceremonies as well as safety and driver meetings will occur before qualification matches. Day 1 runs the longest out of the three days, as it will be the bulk of the qualification matches for the competition..


**Day 2:** The second day of competition is always the most exciting as we move from the final qualification matches and into alliance selection, elimination matches, and awards. Members will be expected to arrive when the doors open at 8am. Qualification matches will resume after opening ceremonies with no safety or driver meetings. After the final qualification match, teams are chosen for alliance selection in order to advance into the playoff matches, followed by a lunch break. Up to three students will be allowed onto the field to represent our team during alliance selection (one of them will almost always be Autumn, the drive coach). After alliance selection, there will be a lunch break, and then playoff matches begin, with breaks in between each round. Awards will be given out between the final matches of the winning two alliances and in the ceremony afterwards; though the awards differ slightly from the awards given out during a regular season.


All team members, unless needed in the pitt or on the drive team, are expected to remain in the stands for opening ceremonies, awards, and during our team’s matches to cheer and support the drive team. Anyone who stays until the very end of the competition will assist in cleaning up the stands, pits, loading the trailer, and unloading everything back at Mountain View.
