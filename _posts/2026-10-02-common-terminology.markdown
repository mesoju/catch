>*Between talking to other teams at competitions and looking at other FRC strategy resources, there are some common terms you’re going to hear that are used to quantify a team’s capabilities or describe a robot’s role during a match.*

**Cycle:** one round of a programmed task; (obtaining a game piece/pieces, scoring, then going to collect another piece/pieces)
* When teams refer to “cycling” during a match, they mean that a bot is continuously gathering game elements and scoring mostly independently.
* Cycle time: how long it takes to complete one cycle
* the shorter, the better!
* Cpm: cycles per minute

**EPA (Expected Points Added):** how much a team scores in an average match
* Cumulative stat; uses past data
* Not good for teams that have done large rebuilds, or trending upward/downward

**OPR (Offensive Power Rating):** average point contribution of a team on alliance
* Better than EPA or plain average because robots on an alliance can be unbalanced
* Robot does not have to physically score pieces, but can help their alliance partners score
* Stabilizes after 8-9 matches
* Only looks at data for current event

**DPR (Defensive Power Rating):** used to determine how defensive a robot is, how much they are able to reduce the opponents’ score
* Always a difficult metric to measure/quantify; I honestly rarely hear people talk about DPR at actual competitions. In terms of measuring how good a team’s defense is, I would recommend focusing on their driving ability/movement around the field (most of what makes good defense is good driving).

**Shuttling/Feeding:** passing game pieces from a neutral/opposing alliance zone to your respective alliance zone or alliance partners for scoring
* Used mostly interchangeably
* Shuttling more accurately refers to robots shooting/driving game pieces to their overall alliance zone to collect and eventually score
* Feeding more accurately refers to delivering game pieces to the human player or a specific bot for them to score
